# Sale Process: During-Sale

**Job:** From handshake to handshake — discovery, demo, trade, numbers, close —
for today's buyer, who arrives with 8+ hours of online research done and one
question: "can I trust this person?"

**Companion skill:** `/objection` — paste what the customer said, get the
framework + words in real time.

---

## 1. The frame

The modern road-to-the-sale is shorter because the customer did half of it
online. Your job is not to present a car; it's to **confirm their research,
fill the gaps, and remove risk.** Trust is the product. The store's process
(TO points, desk workflow) always wins where it differs from this doc.

## 2. Discovery (the most profitable 10 minutes of the deal)

Sell nothing yet. Ask, listen, take visible notes. The goals: motivation,
use-case, decision process, money comfort — in their words.

Core questions (conversational, not interrogation):
- "What's prompting the change — what's the current vehicle not doing?"
  ← their **why**; you'll reuse their exact words at close.
- "Who else will drive it? What's the longest trip it'll take?"
- "What have you already looked at? What did you like / rule out?"
  ← respects their research, reveals competitors still in play.
- "When it comes to the money side, are you a total-price person, a
  payment-per-month person, or a cash person?" ← tells you how to desk it.
- "If we find the right one today, is there anything that would stop you from
  taking it home — anyone else who needs to weigh in?"
  ← surfaces the spouse/co-signer/credit issue NOW, not at the desk.

## 3. Presentation & demo drive

- Present the **three features that map to their discovery answers**, not all
  forty. Family shopper: safety scores, third row, cargo. Truck buyer: payload,
  towing, the specific trailer they mentioned.
- **Always demo.** Ownership feelings start in the driver's seat; a no-demo
  deal is a fragile deal. Preset their radio/seat comfort, have a planned route
  with a highway stretch and a rough patch (this is Michigan — show the ride).
- Shut up during the good parts. Let the vehicle sell.
- Trial closes on the drive, light: "Is this the seat comfort you were missing?"
  "Could you live with this every morning?"

## 4. The trade walk (do it WITH them)

- Walk the trade together, tablet/slip in hand, narrating neutrally what you
  note — no wincing, no lowball theater. They know its flaws; your honesty
  about them builds the case for the number.
- Ask: "What's the story with it — anything been replaced or repaired? What do
  YOU feel it's worth?" (Their number now is negotiating gold later.)
- Plant the reconditioning frame: "We'll put {{recon items}} into it before we
  can retail it — that gap between retail and offer is real cost, not profit."

## 5. Numbers & negotiation

- Present figures **confidently and completely** (vehicle, trade, money down,
  payment) — then be quiet. First one to talk after numbers hit the table
  loses ground.
- Defend value, not price: recap THEIR priorities met, THEIR trade story,
  market data on the unit. Discounting starts only after value is re-anchored.
- Trade objection → the recon frame + "would you pay {{asking}} for it as it
  sits? Neither would our used-car manager."
- Payment objection → work the variables honestly (term, down, unit, rate
  shopping via F&I) — never fudge, never quote payments the desk hasn't blessed.
- Know your TO points and use them early, not as a last rite.

## 6. Closing (asking is the skill)

Trial close all day; ask fully when buying signals stack (possessive language
— "my truck" — logistics questions, silence + re-touching the vehicle).

The asks, escalating:
1. **Assumptive next-step:** "Let me grab the title work started while you two
   pick a delivery day."
2. **Alternative choice:** "Take it tonight, or want it detailed and delivered
   tomorrow?"
3. **Their-words close:** "You said the Traverse died on the school run twice
   this winter. This fixes that today. Should we fix it?"
4. **Summary close:** recap every yes they've already given, then: "What's
   left between you and driving it home?"

**"I need to think about it"** = unresolved concern. Isolate it: "Totally fair.
So I know what to sharpen — is it the payment, the trade number, or the vehicle
itself?" Then handle the real one. If they truly need time: schedule the
decision ("I'll hold the numbers til Thursday at 6 — let's talk then"), never
"call me when you're ready."

## 7. If they leave unsold

Execute the be-back lock from `../lead-engines/floor-and-phone.md` §3: booked
return appointment or scheduled call, photos texted before they exit the lot,
full DriveCentric note same shift (vehicle, motivation, the REAL objection,
next step). Tonight's note quality is next week's be-back close.

## Metrics

- Demo rate (target: 85%+ of shown appointments)
- Write-up rate (sat down to numbers ÷ shown)
- Closing rate (sold ÷ shown — store baseline ~50% on appointments; beat it)
- Real objection logged on every unsold up (binary, honest)
